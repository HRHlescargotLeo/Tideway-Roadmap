What we're proposing

Once a site becomes a public space, its page should be written for someone planning a visit: what's there, how to get there, step-free access, when the lower parts flood, what's on and the art. The engineering story and the construction record stay, further down, for anyone who wants them.

Why

King Edward Memorial Park Foreshore opened on 29 July 2026, adding about 2,600 square metres to the park. Its page on the live site is still built around construction: works updates, working hours and planning documents. That's the record of how the space came about, but it isn't what a visitor needs first.

What's on here

    What's underneath

    Below the park, Tideway built a connection that intercepts one of London's most polluting sewer overflows and diverts it into the tunnel, which passes about 60 metres beneath. Building out into the river gave room to do the work without taking more of the park.

    What the tunnel catches here

    Work began here in 2017. The site page keeps the record: notices, liaison group papers and planning documents.
