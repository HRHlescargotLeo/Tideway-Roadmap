What we're proposing

A visitor's guide to the seven new riverside spaces, with filters for what people care about (step-free access, the parts that flood at high tide, art, play) and a map; one list of what's on, including Active Tideway sessions with links to book; an art trail along the route; and a home for the Tideway Prize.

Why

Both Open Spaces pages still say "we are creating three acres of new public realm" and show renders, although Tideway announced on 4 August that every new space is open. Active Tideway exists only as a press release with two booking links written into the text, and the Tideway Prize as a news story. These are the reasons most Londoners will have to visit the site now, and at the moment they'd struggle to find any of them.

Space names and boroughs are from tideway.london. Features, stations marked [to confirm], event dates and sessions are sample data. Active Tideway's partners, Active360 and London Sports Trust, are real.

Visit the river

Seven new places by the Thames

Building the super sewer meant building out into the river. Those sites are now public spaces, with seating, planting and art. In three of them, parts of the space are designed to flood at high tide.
